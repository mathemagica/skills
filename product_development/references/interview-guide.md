# Discovery Interview Guide

For the person running a product discovery session with a vision owner. Adapt
it to the idea and the person; it is a guide, not a script or a required length.
A 30-45 minute recorded conversation with screen sharing often works well.

## Before The Session

- Prepare the [owner prep note](../templates/owner-prep-note.md) for the
  facilitator to send ahead when practical. The agent drafts it; sending requires
  specific authorization and is not implied by running this guide.
- Agree consent to record, where recordings and transcripts will be stored, which
  AI or transcription services may process them, and who else may see them.
- Agree who owns the idea, the discovery records, the PRD and any code produced
  (see the [workflow contract](workflow-contract.md)).
- Ask the owner to have ready whatever exists: a prototype, the software they use
  today, spreadsheets, diagrams, photos of paper workflows or example documents.
- Check that your recording captures both audio and the shared screen.
- If the owner declines recording, offer notes, a conversation or selected
  screenshots they are comfortable sharing. Recording is optional.

## Opening (about 5 minutes)

- Confirm consent on the recording itself: "Is it still OK that I record this and
  use an AI tool to transcribe and analyze it?"
- Explain the path: this conversation, then a proposed first version for them to
  check, then a requirements document for them to check, then a prototype.
- Ask for the idea in a sentence or two, in their own words. Write down the words
  they use for things; reuse them later instead of technical terms.

## Show, Don't Describe (about 20-30 minutes)

Prefer demonstrations and recent concrete examples over hypotheticals.

- "Walk me through the last time this problem came up. What started it?"
- "Show me how you handle it today, step by step." Ask them to share the actual
  tool, spreadsheet or paper process.
- "Where does it go wrong or take longest? Show me that part."
- For existing software with gaps: "Show me the point where it stops helping you.
  What do you do next?" Note the gap, not the software's whole feature set.
- For a prototype: "Show me what it does today and the part you're proudest of.
  What isn't working yet?"
- For diagrams: "Walk me through this as if I'm the person using it."
- Narrate what is on screen when it matters ("so this column is the waitlist"),
  so the transcript records what the video shows. Note timestamps of key moments.

Then widen the picture:

- "Who would use this? Who would pay for it? Who would set it up and run it?"
  These may be different people.
- "How often does this happen, and what does it cost you when it goes wrong?"
- "What have you already tried?"
- "If the first version could do only one thing, what should it be?"
- "What would make you decide this idea isn't worth pursuing?"

## Closing (about 5 minutes)

Before ending, check you have:

- [ ] The main user, the problem, and today's workaround, shown on screen
- [ ] Who uses, who pays, who operates and who owns the vision
- [ ] The one thing the first version must do
- [ ] Constraints: data involved, devices, offline needs, budget, timeline, rules
- [ ] The riskiest unknown, in their words
- [ ] Anything they want kept out of scope or confidential

Tell them what happens next and roughly when: a short
[assumption check](../templates/assumption-check.md) for them to confirm.

## After The Session

Process the recording only with agreed tools and destinations. Follow
[recording evidence](recording-evidence.md) for transcripts and screen content,
then use [product-discovery-to-scope](../product-discovery-to-scope/SKILL.md).
Keep private recordings and transcripts out of public repositories.
